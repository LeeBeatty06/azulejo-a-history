Tile Designs

Figura Avulsa

Rather than forming one continuous scene, figura avulsa tiles use the individual ceramic square as a picture. Plants, animals, boats, people, and other subjects show an image on each tile while still being able to contribute to an independent image while still belonging to a larger decorative environment. Essentially, every tile is its own little painting.

Padrão

Padrão designs would use repeat patterns. Each tile joins others to create a picture, larger than one tile alone. Repeating patterns of shapes and flowers allowed the building to become part of the design since the full look comes from a wall or surface.

Historic Mural Panels

Large azulejo panels make each tile disappear into a picture. Religious scenes, buildings, landscapes, people, and history turn walls into surfaces that tell stories and decorate spaces.

Floral and Naturalistic Motifs

Flowers, leaves, animals and other natural things became common in ceramics. These natural forms could repeat, mix with shapes or come from art traditions that reached Portugal through textiles, prints, ceramics and other decorations.

Geometric Motifs

Geometry connects some of the tile traditions in Iberia with designs that lasted for centuries. Interlocking shapes and repeating structures make each shape spread rhythmically across buildings showing how repetition can turn a tile into a big visual system.

Contemporary Azulejos

Azulejos are still a part of Portugal's visual environment, and the process of creating Azulejos is still practiced in Portuguese workshops. Contemporary uses reinterpret traditional grids, patterns, imagery and the relationship between ceramics and architecture.
