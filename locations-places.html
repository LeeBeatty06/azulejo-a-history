Notable Locations

  1. São Bento Railway Station, Porto

    The São Bento Railway Station transforms a transportation space into one of Porto's most famous displays of azulejo art. About 20,000 tiles cover the entrance hall, and it is a prime example of a historic mural panel. Its blue and white pictures show events from Portuguese history and daily life. The tiles function as both architectural decoration as well as visual storytelling.

  2. Capela das Almas, Porto

    Capela das Almas from 1929 was designed by Eduardo Leite to mimic the baroque/classic style of the 18th century. It is distinguishable by its exterior covered by blue and white azulejos. This makes the chapel stand out within the surrounding streets. Its panels depict religious subjects and saints, making each separate scene a part of a unified surface.

  3. Igreja de Santo Ildefonso, Porto

    Igreja de Santo Ildefonso is another example of buildings in Porto with blue and white azulejos. The tiles cover the outside and show how azulejos connect imagery with the architecture that it is on.

  4. Palácio da Pena, Sintra

    Palácio da Pena in Sintra shows how azulejos can be part of a building that is already defined by the colors and textures of its exterior. The tilework becomes the component of a larger decorative environment, showing how ceramic art can work with buildings and not just separated.

  5. Igreja de São Lourenço de Almancil, Algarve

    Inside Igreja de São Lourenço de Almancil in Algarve, the blue and white eighteenth century azulejos extend across the church's interior. These tiles are attributed to the tile painter Policarpo de Oliveira Bernardes. The panels depict the life of Saint Lawrence and turn the surrounding architecture into one religious ceramic narrative.

  6. Quinta dos Azulejos, Lisbon

    The Quinta dos Azulejos in Lisbon is a walled courtyard in an oasis of peace and tranquility amongst a bustling city. The main house was originally built in the 17th century. The garden's walls, fountains, columns, and benches are lined with azulejos depicting mythological stories, biblical scenes, hunting, daily life, and exotic animals.
